Model reference
===============

This page states the model as implemented, in the order a Methods section reads
it. Symbols match the code: ``v`` for body velocity, ``omega`` for body angular
velocity, ``R`` for the cloud radius, ``dt`` for the step.

Seal agent
----------

The agent has six degrees of freedom. The body frame is x forward (thrust), y left
(slip), z up (lift); the world frame has z up with depth negative. Orientation is a
yaw, pitch, roll triple with yaw positive to the right, pitch positive nose-up and
roll positive right-side-down, integrated from body rates with
:func:`sealsim.geometry.integrate_orientation` (``ZYX`` Euler sequence, yaw and
pitch sign-flipped). Velocities come from one of three sources:

* a six-vector ``(thrust, slip, lift, yaw, pitch, roll)`` in m/s and rad/s;
* one of the 13 five-dimensional prototypical movements (PMs) in
  ``sealsim/data/prototypical_movements/pm5d_k13.csv``;
* a dive generated by :func:`sealsim.dive.generate_dive`: a Markov chain over the
  50 two-dimensional PMs (thrust, slip, yaw) with the start and transition tables,
  depth and pitch from the U or V dive phases (MINOSplus Fig. 16, logger rate
  0.2 Hz, zero-order hold, Gaussian smoothing of 0.4 s), floor and surface
  switches.

Particle cloud
--------------

Particles are uniform in the ball of radius ``R`` around the seal. The radius is the
visibility reach of the largest grain, ``R = a_max / tan(acuity)``; each particle
carries its own reach ``d_i = a_i / tan(acuity)`` so that small grains vanish first.
Grain diameters are uniform in the chosen range. Two vision presets are used:

================  ==========  ================  =========
preset            acuity      grain diameter    R
================  ==========  ================  =========
``north_sea``     0.27 deg    0.5 to 2 mm       0.42 m
``thesis``        5.5 arcmin  20 to 160 um      0.10 m
================  ==========  ================  =========

The cloud is specified either by a particle count or by a number density in
m\ :sup:`-3`; :func:`sealsim.cloud.number_density_from_spm` converts suspended
matter (g/m\ :sup:`3`) to a number density for quartz grains of the given sizes.
Particles drift with the current, the seal moves through them, and a particle that
leaves the ball re-enters by point reflection of its pre-step position, which keeps
the density exact and the cloud seed-stable on both compute backends.

Step duration
-------------

Unless ``dt`` is given, a step lasts ``min(0.5 R / |v|, pi / |omega|)``: the seal
never crosses more than half the cloud in one sample and never turns by more than
half a revolution, so the two samples of every visible particle lie on one
unambiguous great circle.

Optic flow
----------

For each particle the direction before and after the step is expressed in the body
frame of its own sample. Flow is the great-circle displacement between the two
directions, reported as a magnitude in radians and as components along the local
azimuth and elevation tangents. The visual field is azimuth within 104 deg of the
nose and elevation between -12 and +69 deg.

Readouts
--------

All three run on every step, decision logic lives in the figures:

Focus of expansion
    Every flow vector lies on the great circle through its particle and the focus
    of expansion, so the plane normal ``n_i = u_i x t_i`` is orthogonal to it. The
    focus is the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of ``sum(n_i n_i^T)``,
    signed so flow points away from it. The heading error is its angle to the true
    translation direction.

Depth order
    Spearman rank correlation between inverse flow magnitude and particle distance
    inside the visual field: 1 means flow orders the particles perfectly by depth.

Translation-rotation index
    The same cloud is run with translation only and with rotation only; the index
    is ``(T - R) / (T + R)`` of the summed flow magnitudes, +1 for pure translation,
    -1 for pure rotation. The critical rotation rate at which it crosses zero for an
    object abeam is ``omega_crit = v / d``.

Compute backends
----------------

Array code runs on numpy or on CuPy (``--backend numpy|cupy|auto``). Clouds are
sampled in numpy and moved to the device, so a seed means the same particles on both.
Small 3-vectors, the scipy rotation and the 3 x 3 focus eigenproblem stay on the CPU.
